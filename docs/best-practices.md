# Best practices

- **One source at a time.** Connect, small ingest, `dream --dry-run`, `dream`,
  read the brief. Then the next source. Five at once is undebuggable.
- **Dry-run before every dream that matters.** It prices the pass and shows the
  bundles the model will see. Surprises belong in preview, not in bills.
- **Read the gate before widening.** `memcal gatecheck` tells you whether a new
  source's traffic is signal or noise shaped like signal. Tune senders (`memcal
  senders`), top-tier people (`memcal top`), and mutes before paying for a
  full pass.
- **Mute for noise, block for never.** Muting keeps archive rows and stops model
  spend; blocking drops the sender entirely. Platform mutes are recorded, not
  fought.
- **Correct in the open, in the day.** A wrong row fixed with a typed write now
  beats a perfect dream tonight — and the correction survives the night. Never
  let a wrong answer sit because "dream will fix it."
- **Name people early.** `memcal who --adopt` is free; an afternoon of `--confirm`
  and `--split` beats a month of misattributed rows. Contacts-backed identities
  outrank all future guesses.
- **Keep the wiki lazy.** Pages earn existence from evidence, not from contact
  imports. Facts on pages, obligations in to-dos, uncertainty in questions —
  each in its typed home.
- **Schedule the night job.** `memcal schedule install` makes freshness and dream
  automatic: catch-up collection on wake, the full pass when owed, cheap checks
  otherwise.
- **Review weekly.** `memcal review` plus the Runs tab: what it wrote, what it
  asks, what it could not resolve. Ten minutes here prevents zombie to-dos and
  stale pages.
- **Measure plumbing free, quality deliberately.** The integration benchmark runs
  in a second on every change; the model layer and the seven-day experiment spend
  real money and run on declaration, frozen corpora, and held-out lives.
